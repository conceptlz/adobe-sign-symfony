    An independent project

    conceptlz/adobe-sign (the framework-agnostic PHP SDK), conceptlz/adobe-sign-laravel (its Laravel bridge) and conceptlz/adobe-sign-symfony (its Symfony bundle) are created and maintained by Conceptualize. They are client libraries: they call the public Acrobat Sign REST API v6 on your behalf, using credentials you obtain from Adobe for your own Acrobat Sign account.

    None of the packages is an Adobe product. Adobe Inc. has not developed, reviewed, certified, sponsored or endorsed them, and is not responsible for them. Adobe's own documentation and support channels are the authority on how Acrobat Sign behaves. When this documentation and Adobe's disagree, Adobe's describes the service, and this documentation describes what the SDK does with it.

    The SDK's models are generated from the OpenAPI description of the REST API that Adobe publishes, and its behaviour was checked against a real Acrobat Sign account. That makes the SDK compatible with the API; it does not make it official.

    Not legal advice

    Signature law is jurisdiction-specific

    The pages on signature levels, identity checks, eIDAS and national signature laws explain how these frameworks are generally understood, so that developers and technical leads can talk to their lawyers in the same terms. They are not legal advice and do not create any professional relationship.

    Whether a particular signature is valid, admissible or sufficient depends on the jurisdiction, the type of document, the parties, any contractual requirements and the facts. The SDK lets you request a signature level and reports the evidence Adobe returns. It cannot decide what the law requires, and whether a signature is qualified depends on the trust service provider configured on your Acrobat Sign account, which the SDK does not control. Confirm your requirements with qualified counsel or your compliance team.

    Your responsibilities

    When you build with these packages, you remain responsible for:

    • Your Adobe relationship. Your use of Acrobat Sign is governed by your agreement with Adobe, including its terms of use, pricing, plan limits and acceptable-use rules.
    • Credentials. Integration Keys, OAuth client secrets and tokens give access to your account. Store them securely; the SDK redacts them from its exceptions and logs, but it cannot protect a key you commit to a repository.
    • Personal data. Agreements contain the names, email addresses and documents of real people. You decide what you send, you are typically the data controller, and you need a lawful basis, appropriate notices and retention rules. See the privacy policy for what the packages themselves do with data.
    • Signers' experience and consent. Choosing identity checks, signature levels and signing order is a business and legal decision as much as a technical one.
    • Verifying outcomes. Check agreement status and signature evidence before you treat a contract as concluded.

    Third-party services can change

    Acrobat Sign is a service operated by Adobe. Adobe can change its API, its behaviour, its error codes and the features available on each plan at any time. Several behaviours documented here, such as data-centre routing, ETag handling and fields that differ from the published specification, were observed against a real account on the dates given in the changelog and may change. Features such as hybrid routing, digital signatures, government-ID checks and identity gateways also depend on your account's plan and settings.

    Conceptualize has no control over Adobe's service and cannot guarantee its availability, pricing or continued compatibility. When Adobe changes something, an SDK update may be needed.
